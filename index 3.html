1. How IP geolocation works

Every device on the internet is reached through a public IP address. IP addresses are handed out in blocks to ISPs and organisations by regional registries (AFRINIC, ARIN, RIPE and others). Geolocation providers build databases that map those blocks to places. They combine registry records, ISP-supplied data, network routing and latency measurements, and sometimes location data collected from apps and Wi-Fi networks. A lookup finds the block that contains the address and returns the place linked to it.

Accuracy and limitations

  • Country level is usually reliable (roughly 95-99%).
  • City level is often right for fixed broadband, but frequently wrong by tens of kilometres.
  • Mobile networks are the weakest case. Carrier traffic often exits through a gateway in a different city from the user.
  • VPNs, proxies and Tor show the exit server's location, not the real one.
  • Carrier-grade NAT puts many customers behind one shared address, so the result describes the carrier, not the person.
  • Stale data. Blocks get re-assigned and databases lag behind, so results can be outdated.
  • No street address. The coordinates returned are typically the centre of a city or region, which is why the map shows an uncertainty circle.
  • Privacy and consent. The target never has to grant permission, so this method must only be used on devices you own or are authorised to track.

2. GPS versus cell tower triangulation

AspectGPS (satellites)Cell tower triangulation
How it worksThe phone receives timing signals from at least four satellites and calculates its position.The network measures signal strength, timing or angle to several towers and estimates where the phone sits between them.
Typical accuracyAbout 3-10 m in open sky.About 50 m to 1 km in cities, several km in rural areas.
Indoors / urban canyonsWeak. Buildings block or reflect the signal.Works indoors, as long as there is network coverage.
Time to first fixSeconds to a minute (faster with assisted GPS).Near instant.
Battery useHigher, the GPS chip runs continuously.Lower, the phone is already talking to towers.
Needs data or networkNo. Satellites work without a SIM or data plan.Yes. Needs cellular coverage.
Who controls itThe device and the user's permission.The network operator, which can locate a phone without app permission.
Coverage gapsTunnels, dense forest, deep indoors.Remote areas with few towers.

What this app uses

The browser's Geolocation API picks the best source on its own. With high accuracy on, a phone uses GPS. Otherwise, or indoors, the browser falls back to Wi-Fi and cell data. The accuracy value in the GPS tab shows the radius, in metres, within which the browser is 68% sure the device sits.

3. Privacy and ethics

Location is sensitive personal data. Track only people who have agreed, tell them clearly what is collected, store as little as possible, and delete it when it is no longer needed. In Uganda, the Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019 treats personal data, including location, as requiring consent and a lawful purpose.
